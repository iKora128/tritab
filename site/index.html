Gestures

One swipe, one tab.

All with three fingers. Slow drifts and re-placed fingers don’t trigger anything, and two-finger scrolling won’t flip a page back by accident while you swipe.

Each gesture presses the app’s own shortcut for you. The keys above are for Safari and Chrome; in Firefox and VS Code, for example, TriTab sends ⌥⌘→ instead.

Use Three Finger Drag? Switch TriTab to four fingers in Settings, and every gesture uses one more finger.

Notch Shelf

Drag files, or images, links and text from your browser, up to the notch to park them for later. One switch decides whether files are added as references or copies. Drop several files at once and they become one stack. Right-click for Quick Look or Show in Finder. The shelf keeps its items after a restart, and can clear out old ones for you.

Swipe while dragging

Hold a window or file with one finger and swipe with three others to move to the next desktop. Whatever you’re holding comes along.

⌘ alone for Eisu / Kana

For Japanese input. Press left ⌘ by itself for Eisu and right ⌘ for Kana, like the popular Karabiner-Elements rule. A four-finger tap switches between them too.

Five fingers to sleep

Swipe up with five fingers to put the display, or the whole Mac, to sleep right away. Handy when you step away from your desk.

Why TriTab

Small, private, and open.

13 MB

Light

About 13 MB of memory while it runs. The settings window opens in its own process and quits when you close it.

Private

Touches and keys are read on your Mac only to recognize gestures. Nothing is recorded or sent. The only network request is the update check.

Signed and notarized

Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple. Updates arrive on their own and, if you allow it, install while you’re away.

Open source

MIT licensed. Read the code, open an issue, or build it yourself with just the Command Line Tools.

Get started

Up and running in a minute.

  1. 1

    Install

    Open the DMG and drag TriTab to Applications, or install it with Homebrew.

  2. 2

    Allow Accessibility

    TriTab needs it to send shortcuts like ⌃Tab and ⌘W to the app you’re using. It asks on first launch.

  3. 3

    Free up three fingers

    Move macOS’s own three-finger gestures to four fingers. TriTab shows you where, and tells you if anything still conflicts.

FAQ

Questions

Can I use it alongside BetterTouchTool or other gesture apps?

Yes. If the same gesture is set up in both, though, both will act on it, so turn it off in one of them. TriTab tells you when macOS’s own trackpad gestures overlap with its own.

I use Three Finger Drag. Can I still use TriTab?

Yes. Switch TriTab to four fingers in Settings. Every gesture then uses one more finger.

Why does it need Accessibility access?

To send keyboard shortcuts such as ⌃Tab and ⌘W to the app in front, and to notice clicks and the ⌘ key. What it reads never leaves your Mac.

Why isn’t it on the Mac App Store?

TriTab reads the trackpad through a private macOS framework, which the App Store doesn’t allow. The download is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit TriTab from its menu bar icon and move it to the Trash. If you installed it with Homebrew, run brew uninstall --cask tritab (add --zap to remove its settings too).
